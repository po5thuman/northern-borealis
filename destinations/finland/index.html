Northern Lights in Finland

Plan an aurora trip to Finnish Lapland and compare Rovaniemi, Levi, the best time to see the Northern Lights and the Arctic experiences that make Finland unique.

Finland is one of Europe's classic Northern Lights destinations. In the country's far north, Finnish Lapland combines long winter nights and sparsely populated Arctic landscapes with established resorts, organised aurora experiences and some of Finland's best-known winter attractions.

For most travellers planning to see the aurora borealis in Finland, the main decision is where in Lapland to stay. Rovaniemi combines a broad choice of Northern Lights tours with Santa Claus Village and the facilities of Lapland's regional capital, while Levi combines aurora excursions with skiing, snowmobiling and a compact resort atmosphere.

This country guide focuses on Northern Lights in Finland: where to go, when to travel and how the main Finnish destinations differ. For tour comparisons and booking options across the region, continue to our dedicated Northern Lights tours in Lapland guide.

Where Is Finland?

Finland is a country in Northern Europe, bordered by Sweden to the west, Norway to the north and Russia to the east, with the Baltic Sea to the south. Its northernmost region, Finnish Lapland, extends across and beyond the Arctic Circle and is the main focus for Northern Lights travel in Finland.

So where in Finland is Lapland? It covers the northern part of the country and includes many of Finland's best-known Arctic destinations. Two particularly useful bases for Northern Lights travellers are Rovaniemi, the regional capital on the Arctic Circle, and Levi, one of Finland's major ski resorts.

Both provide organised aurora excursions and easy access to darker countryside, but they suit different styles of trip. Our Lapland Northern Lights guide compares the two destinations in more detail.

Is Finland Scandinavian?

Finland is one of the five Nordic countries, alongside Sweden, Norway, Denmark and Iceland. In the narrower geographical and cultural usage, Scandinavia generally refers to Denmark, Norway and Sweden, so Finland is Nordic rather than Scandinavian.

For travellers planning a Northern Lights trip, Finland nevertheless shares many of the practical advantages of its northern neighbours: Arctic landscapes, winter activities and access to dark northern skies. You can compare it with our destination guides to Norway and Sweden.

What Is Finland Known For?

Beyond the Northern Lights, Finland has a distinctive combination of Arctic landscapes, winter traditions and outdoor experiences that makes it particularly well suited to a multi-day winter trip.

Lapland and Arctic winter. Northern Finland is known for snowy forests, frozen lakes, reindeer, long winter nights and activities ranging from husky experiences and snowmobiling to skiing and glass-roofed accommodation.

Santa Claus and Rovaniemi. Santa Claus Village is located in Rovaniemi on the Arctic Circle, making the city especially popular with families and travellers planning a Christmas-season trip.

Sauna and nature. Sauna is deeply associated with Finnish culture, while the country's extensive forests and lakes are central to its outdoor identity throughout the year.

Santa Claus Village in Rovaniemi

One of the biggest reasons travellers choose Rovaniemi is Santa Claus Village. The attraction sits on the Arctic Circle just outside central Rovaniemi and can easily be combined with a Northern Lights tour later in the day.

Visitors can meet Santa Claus, cross the marked Arctic Circle and visit the Santa Claus Main Post Office. This makes Rovaniemi particularly convenient for families and anyone who wants to combine an aurora trip with the traditional Lapland Christmas experience.

Best Time to See the Northern Lights in Finland

The practical Northern Lights season in Finland runs from late August into early April, when northern Finland has sufficiently dark nights for aurora viewing. September through March is the main period for Northern Lights travel in Finnish Lapland.

Winter brings the snow-covered scenery most travellers associate with Lapland, while autumn and early spring can provide aurora viewing without the deepest part of the Arctic winter. Whatever month you choose, staying for several nights gives you more opportunities to encounter a suitable combination of aurora activity, clear skies and local weather.

The Christmas period is particularly popular in Rovaniemi because visitors can combine winter activities and Santa Claus Village with evening Northern Lights tours. Levi, meanwhile, is particularly attractive during the ski season for travellers who want to combine time on the slopes with aurora excursions.

Why Choose Finland for the Aurora Borealis?

Finland works particularly well for travellers who want the Northern Lights to be part of a broader Arctic holiday rather than the only reason for the trip.

Easy access to dark Arctic landscapes. Much of Finnish Lapland is sparsely populated, and established destinations such as Rovaniemi and Levi provide organised excursions into darker countryside away from the brightest local lighting.

A wide choice of aurora experiences. Depending on where you stay, you can choose from dedicated Northern Lights chases, photography tours, minivan excursions, snowmobile trips and other winter experiences. Compare the main destinations in our Northern Lights tours in Lapland guide.

More than just the Northern Lights. Husky experiences, reindeer activities, snowmobiling, skiing, saunas, glass-roofed accommodation and snowy Arctic landscapes make it easy to build a complete winter holiday around your aurora evenings.

Two different main bases. Rovaniemi works particularly well for travellers interested in Santa Claus Village and a broad selection of organised activities, while Levi is particularly attractive for ski holidays and winter sports.
