Northern Lights in Sweden: Where & When to See Them

Swedish Lapland combines dark Arctic skies with two standout aurora bases: Abisko, famous for its unusually favourable local weather, and Kiruna, a convenient hub for Northern Lights tours and winter adventures.

Compare Abisko & Kiruna

Sweden is one of Europe's classic Northern Lights destinations, and the country's far north gives aurora travellers two particularly strong choices. Abisko is known for dark skies and a local weather pattern that can leave it clearer than surrounding areas, while Kiruna combines excellent aurora access with more accommodation, restaurants and Arctic activities.

Both sit in Swedish Lapland, where the long nights of autumn and winter create a broad viewing season. Around Kiruna, the Northern Lights are generally visible from September through March, with sightings sometimes possible from late August into April when darkness and weather cooperate. Seeing them still depends on two things you cannot schedule perfectly: aurora activity and a sufficiently clear sky.

This guide explains where to see the Northern Lights in Sweden, when to go, whether Abisko or Kiruna suits your trip better, and how to improve your chances once you're there. If you want to understand the phenomenon itself first, read our guide to what causes the Northern Lights.

Abisko vs Kiruna: Which Is Better for Your Trip?

You do not need to treat this as a simple “winner.” Abisko and Kiruna solve different travel problems. Abisko is the stronger fit when seeing the aurora is the central purpose of the trip. Kiruna is the more versatile base when you want the Northern Lights alongside restaurants, a wider choice of accommodation and other Arctic experiences.

Why Swedish Lapland Is So Good for the Northern Lights

You're far enough north for frequent aurora opportunities. Kiruna and Abisko lie deep in Arctic Sweden, in a region well positioned for Northern Lights viewing during the dark season. You do not need an extreme geomagnetic storm to have a chance of seeing an aurora this far north.

Abisko has an unusual local weather advantage. The mountains around Abisko can create a patch of clearer sky even when nearby areas are cloudy. This phenomenon is commonly called the “blue hole of Abisko” and is a major reason the area has become internationally known for aurora tourism.

You can build a full winter trip around the aurora. Swedish Lapland is not only about waiting for darkness. Dog sledding, snowmobiling, skiing, Sámi cultural experiences and the ICEHOTEL near Kiruna make it easy to fill the daylight hours while keeping the evenings free for aurora hunting.

Abisko and Kiruna work well together. Abisko lies about 100 kilometres west of Kiruna, so travellers do not necessarily have to choose only one. Kiruna can work as the transport and activity hub while Abisko adds a more dedicated dark-sky experience.

Northern Lights in Abisko

Abisko is the place to look at first if the Northern Lights are the main reason you're travelling to Sweden. Abisko National Park is surrounded by mountains and is known for its unusually favourable local weather. Even when cloud affects surrounding areas, a clearer patch can sometimes persist over Abisko.

The best-known viewing attraction is Aurora Sky Station on Mount Nuolja, reached by chairlift when operating. Its elevated position, dark surroundings and panoramic outlook make it a distinctive place to spend an aurora night, although — like every Northern Lights experience — clear skies and aurora activity can never be guaranteed.

Abisko itself is much smaller than Kiruna, which is part of the appeal. It feels like an aurora destination rather than a city break. For the actual experiences available there, see our best Northern Lights tours in Abisko.

Northern Lights in Kiruna

Kiruna is Sweden's northernmost city and one of the easiest bases for a broader Swedish Lapland adventure. It offers hotels, restaurants and transport connections while keeping Arctic wilderness close enough that aurora tours can leave the brighter urban area behind.

Kiruna is particularly attractive if your trip includes more than aurora hunting. Nearby Jukkasjärvi is home to the world-famous ICEHOTEL, and the wider region offers dog sledding, snowmobiling and other winter activities. Abisko is just over an hour away by road or rail, so some visitors use Kiruna as their main base and add an Abisko excursion.

The tour selection is also varied: you can find straightforward aurora chases, photography-focused nights, food experiences and excursions toward Abisko. Compare them in our guide to the best Northern Lights tours in Kiruna.

Best Time to See the Northern Lights in Sweden

For Swedish Lapland, a practical Northern Lights season is September through March. Around Kiruna, sightings can sometimes begin in late August and continue into April, but the core season is the period when the nights are reliably dark enough for aurora viewing.

September–October: A good choice if you want aurora viewing without the deepest winter conditions. Kiruna Lapland notes that September and October can produce particularly strong and vivid displays.

November–February: The longest nights of the season give you plenty of darkness. These months also deliver the classic snowy Arctic landscape, but they bring colder temperatures and popular winter travel periods.

March: One of the most appealing compromises. Kiruna Lapland highlights March as another strong aurora month, while the returning daylight and somewhat milder conditions can make daytime activities more comfortable.

There is no single month that guarantees the Northern Lights. Cloud cover can matter as much as aurora activity, which is why staying several nights is more useful than trying to identify one supposedly perfect date. How to See the Northern Lights in Sweden

You can see the aurora independently or join a guided tour. Neither approach can guarantee a display, but they suit different travellers.

Join a guided tour if you want local help with conditions and logistics. Guides can react to cloud cover, choose dark viewing locations and handle winter driving. Depending on the excursion, thermal clothing, hot drinks, photography help or professional photos may also be included. This is usually the simplest option for a first trip.

Go independently if you value flexibility. With suitable winter-driving experience and a safe place away from artificial light, you can watch without a fixed schedule and stay as long as conditions remain worthwhile. Check both cloud forecasts and aurora conditions rather than relying on the Kp index alone.

Give yourself more than one opportunity. The Northern Lights are a natural phenomenon, and Swedish Lapland can still be cloudy. A multi-night stay gives changing weather and aurora activity time to work in your favour.

Frequently Asked Questions

✨ Can you see the Northern Lights in Sweden?
Yes. Northern Sweden lies beneath the auroral oval, and Swedish Lapland offers excellent opportunities to see the Northern Lights when skies are dark and clear. Abisko and Kiruna are two of the best-known bases for aurora trips.
📍 Where is the best place to see the Northern Lights in Sweden?
Abisko is one of Sweden's best-known Northern Lights destinations because the surrounding mountains often help produce clearer skies than nearby areas. Kiruna is another excellent choice, particularly if you want a larger base, more Arctic activities and easy access to both Abisko and Jukkasjärvi.
🗓️ When can you see the Northern Lights in Sweden?
In far northern Sweden, the main season runs from September through March. Around Kiruna, aurora can sometimes be visible from late August into April when the sky is sufficiently dark. See our full timing guide for more detail.
⚖️ Is Abisko or Kiruna better for the Northern Lights?
Abisko is especially attractive if your trip is focused primarily on aurora viewing and dark-sky conditions. Kiruna suits travellers who want more accommodation, restaurants and Arctic activities while still having excellent Northern Lights opportunities and easy access to Abisko.
🚐 Do you need a tour to see the Northern Lights in Sweden?
No. You can see the Northern Lights independently when conditions are right. Guided tours can be useful because local guides can respond to cloud cover, choose dark viewing locations and help with winter logistics and photography.
🌌 How many nights should I stay in Swedish Lapland?
There is no guaranteed number because both aurora activity and cloud cover vary. Giving yourself several nights rather than relying on a single evening gives you more opportunities for suitable conditions.
