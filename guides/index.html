Northern Lights Guides

Plan your aurora trip from start to finish — when to go, where to go, how to find the lights, how to photograph them and what is actually happening in the sky above you.

Seeing the Northern Lights is one of those travel experiences that rewards good planning. The aurora itself cannot be scheduled, but choosing the right season, staying in a strong aurora destination and allowing several possible viewing nights can give you more opportunities to get beneath a clear, dark sky.

Start with the essentials: when should you go, where should you go, how do you actually find the lights, and what causes them? Once the trip is planned, our photography guides can help you work out how to capture the aurora with either a dedicated camera or an iPhone. You can also use our Northern Lights destination guides for country-level planning or go directly to our Northern Lights tours directory to compare tours from individual Arctic cities and regions.

If you're completely new to aurora travel, don't worry about trying to understand everything at once. The simplest route is to choose your dates first, narrow down the destination second, and then work out how you want to hunt the lights once you're there.

Choose Your Northern Lights Destination

The auroral oval stretches across several of Europe's northernmost regions, but each destination offers a different kind of trip. Norway combines several distinct Arctic bases with a large selection of guided aurora experiences, Sweden pairs inland Arctic conditions with famous viewing locations such as Abisko, Iceland makes it easy to combine the lights with a wider sightseeing trip, and Finnish Lapland combines aurora hunting with classic winter activities and distinctive accommodation.

Norway

Choose Norway for a broad variety of aurora experiences and very different Arctic bases — from lively Tromsø and quiet Alta to the mountain-and-fjord scenery around Svolvær in the Lofoten Islands and the High Arctic environment of Svalbard.

Iceland

Choose Iceland if the Northern Lights are one part of a broader winter trip. Reykjavik is the country's main base for organised aurora tours, while Akureyri offers a smaller northern base with quick access to darker surroundings.

Reykjavík Tours Akureyri Tours

Sweden

Choose Swedish Lapland for inland Arctic landscapes and two very different bases. Abisko is especially known for its local microclimate, while Kiruna combines aurora viewing with a broader range of Lapland activities and transport connections.

Abisko Tours Kiruna Tours

Finland

Choose Finnish Lapland if you want to build a complete winter holiday around the aurora. Rovaniemi combines Northern Lights tours with a large choice of winter activities, while Levi pairs aurora hunting with skiing, snowmobiling and a quieter resort setting.

Northern Lights Trip Planning FAQs

🧭 What should I plan first for a Northern Lights trip?
Start with the time of year and destination. You need sufficiently dark nights, a location within or near the auroral oval, and enough time in the destination to give yourself multiple opportunities for clear skies. After choosing when and where to go, compare local Northern Lights tours and plan the rest of your itinerary around them.
🚙 Should I book a Northern Lights tour or go hunting on my own?
Both are possible. Independent aurora hunting gives you flexibility, but a guided tour is often easier for first-time visitors because local guides follow cloud forecasts, know suitable dark-sky locations and handle winter driving. Travellers who are comfortable driving in Arctic conditions can also hunt independently with reliable weather and aurora forecasts.
🌌 How many nights should I allow for seeing the Northern Lights?
There is no guaranteed number because cloud cover and aurora activity vary from night to night. Staying for several nights gives you more opportunities than relying on a single evening. If seeing the aurora is a major reason for your trip, build multiple possible viewing nights into the itinerary.
📍 Which Northern Lights destination should I choose?
The right destination depends on the trip you want. Tromsø has a large selection of aurora tours, Abisko is known for its local clear-sky microclimate, Iceland combines aurora viewing with a broad sightseeing itinerary, the Lofoten Islands offer dramatic photography locations, and Finnish Lapland is popular for winter activities and glass-roof accommodation.
