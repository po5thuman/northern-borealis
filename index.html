Northern Lights Travel FAQs

A few of the most important things to know before planning an aurora trip in Europe.

🌍 Where are the best places to see the Northern Lights in Europe?
Popular Northern Lights destinations in Europe include Tromsø, Alta, Svalbard and the Lofoten Islands in Norway; Reykjavík and Akureyri in Iceland; Abisko and Kiruna in Sweden; and Rovaniemi and Levi in Finnish Lapland. The right destination depends on the type of trip, accessibility, weather and winter activities you want. See our best places to see the Northern Lights guide for a detailed comparison.
📅 When is the best time to see the Northern Lights in Europe?
The main Northern Lights travel season across northern Europe generally runs from late August or September into early April, when nights are dark enough for aurora viewing. Conditions vary by destination and month, and clear skies are essential. Our best time to see the Northern Lights guide compares the season in more detail.
✨ Do Northern Lights tours guarantee you will see the aurora?
No Northern Lights tour can guarantee that the aurora will appear because displays depend on solar activity, cloud cover and local weather. Guided tours can improve the practical side of the hunt by using local forecasts, travelling to darker locations and searching for clearer skies.
🌌 How many nights should I stay for the Northern Lights?
There is no fixed number of nights that guarantees a sighting. Staying for several nights gives you more opportunities to encounter clear skies and suitable aurora conditions than relying on a single evening. If seeing the Northern Lights is a major reason for the trip, build several possible viewing nights into your itinerary.
🚐 Do I need to book a Northern Lights tour?
A tour is not essential, but it can be useful for first-time visitors. Local guides monitor weather and cloud conditions, know suitable viewing locations and handle winter driving. Travellers comfortable with Arctic conditions can also search for the Northern Lights independently. See our guide to seeing the Northern Lights for more practical advice.
