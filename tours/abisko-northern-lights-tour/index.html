The 5 Best Northern Lights Tours in Abisko

Compare small-group aurora chases, photography tours, snowmobile adventures and Sámi experiences in one of Swedish Lapland's best-known Northern Lights destinations.

Compare Abisko Tours

Abisko has built a worldwide reputation among aurora travellers for one simple reason: clear sky matters just as much as solar activity, and this small corner of Swedish Lapland is unusually good at producing it. Abisko sits between mountains beside Lake Torneträsk, where a local weather pattern can leave clearer sky over the area even when nearby parts of northern Sweden are cloudier.

That doesn't make the Northern Lights guaranteed — nowhere can promise that — but it does make Abisko an excellent base for an aurora-focused trip. The village is small, the surrounding landscape is dark, and you can choose between straightforward guided chases and more distinctive nights involving photography, snowmobiles or Sámi culture.

We've compared five Abisko Northern Lights experiences below by type of experience, rating, price, duration and inclusions. If you're still deciding where in Sweden to stay, see our broader Northern Lights in Sweden guide, or compare Abisko with nearby Kiruna Northern Lights tours.

Which Abisko Northern Lights Tour Should You Choose?

Choose the Sámi Concert and Northern Lights Tour if you want the most complete evening rather than a pure chase. It costs more, but the cultural component, photography and eight-person cap make it a different kind of experience from the other four.

Choose the snowmobile tour if you want an active Arctic experience. It turns the journey away from the lights of the village into part of the night rather than simply transport to a viewing location.

Choose the small-group van chase if photography is your priority. You still get the flexibility of a mobile chase, but with specific help capturing the aurora.

Choose the autumn chase if you're visiting early in the season, while the classic guided chase is the simplest fit if you mainly want transport, local guidance and a relatively low starting price.

Why Is Abisko So Good for the Northern Lights?

Abisko's reputation isn't based on stronger auroras magically appearing over the village. Its advantage is primarily about viewing conditions.

The “blue hole of Abisko.” Abisko lies among mountains near Lake Torneträsk. The local topography can create a patch of clearer sky over the area when nearby places are cloudier — the phenomenon commonly nicknamed the “blue hole.” That matters because even a strong aurora is useless to a traveller if a solid cloud layer is blocking the view.

Dark surroundings. Abisko is a tiny settlement beside a national park rather than a major city. Once you're away from the village lights, the landscape provides the darkness needed to see weaker aurora displays more clearly.

Mobility still helps. Abisko's microclimate improves the appeal of staying here, but it doesn't eliminate weather. Guided chases can respond to cloud conditions and move when another location offers a better opening. For the fundamentals behind forecasts, darkness and solar activity, see our guide to how to see the Northern Lights and our live aurora forecast.

Best Time to See the Northern Lights in Abisko

The usable aurora season around Abisko stretches from late August into April, once the bright summer nights have ended. For most winter travellers, the core season is autumn through early spring, with mid-November to mid-March providing especially long periods of darkness.

Don't treat a particular month as a guarantee, though. On any individual night, you still need darkness, enough auroral activity and — critically — a break in the cloud. A clear September or March night can beat a cloudy night in the middle of winter.

If the Northern Lights are the main reason for your trip, staying for several nights gives you more opportunities to encounter the right combination of weather and activity. See our month-by-month Northern Lights guide for the wider seasonal picture.

Abisko vs Kiruna for the Northern Lights

Abisko and Kiruna are close enough to combine, but they make different bases. Abisko is the more aurora-focused choice: it's smaller, darker and internationally known for its clear-sky microclimate. Kiruna is the more practical town base, with a wider selection of services, accommodation and Arctic activities.

If your trip is built primarily around maximizing nights under dark skies, Abisko is an obvious place to spend at least part of it. If you want Northern Lights hunting alongside a broader Swedish Lapland itinerary, Kiruna can be easier logistically. Many travellers don't need to choose one exclusively — Abisko is roughly 100 km west of Kiruna and the two are connected by road and rail.

Where to Stay in Abisko

Staying in Abisko puts you close to the national park, Lake Torneträsk and the Aurora Sky Station area. Because the village is small, accommodation choice is much more limited than in Kiruna, so it makes sense to secure your room once your travel dates are fixed.

A stay here also gives you one major advantage over visiting only for a tour: you can keep watching the sky before and after your booked activity. On a clear night, you may not need to travel far from your accommodation to find a dark view.

Frequently Asked Questions

🌌 Is Abisko a good place to see the Northern Lights?
Yes. Abisko is widely regarded as one of Sweden's strongest Northern Lights destinations. Its position among the surrounding mountains often gives the area clearer skies than nearby locations, while the dark landscape around Abisko National Park provides excellent viewing conditions.
💙 Why is Abisko famous for the Northern Lights?
Abisko is known for a local weather phenomenon often called the “blue hole of Abisko.” The surrounding mountains can help create a patch of clearer sky over the area even when nearby locations are cloudier, which is one reason Abisko has become internationally known for aurora viewing.
🗓️ What is the best time to see the Northern Lights in Abisko?
Northern Lights can be seen around Abisko from late August into April when skies are dark enough. Mid-November to mid-March offers especially long, dark nights, but clear skies and aurora activity matter more than the calendar on any individual night.
📍 Is Abisko or Kiruna better for Northern Lights tours?
Abisko is especially attractive if your priority is dark skies and its well-known local microclimate, while Kiruna offers a larger town base, more accommodation and a wider range of Arctic activities. The two are close enough that many travellers combine them in one Swedish Lapland trip. Compare our Kiruna tours here.
⏰ Should I book an Abisko Northern Lights tour in advance?
Booking ahead is sensible if your dates are fixed, particularly for small-group, photography, snowmobile and other limited-capacity experiences. Availability can become tighter during popular winter travel periods.
