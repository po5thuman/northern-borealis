Akureyri offers a very different way to chase the Northern Lights in Iceland. Set at the head of Eyjafjörður in the north of the country, the town is surrounded by mountains, open countryside and dark roads that guides can use to search for clearer skies away from local light pollution.

There are fewer Northern Lights tours in Akureyri than you'll find in Reykjavik, but the choice covers the formats most travellers actually need: a dedicated photography experience, a guided tour with a second-chance option, and a shorter evening excursion. That makes Akureyri especially appealing if North Iceland is already part of your itinerary and you'd rather chase the aurora without returning south to the capital.

Why See the Northern Lights from Akureyri?

Akureyri is one of the main bases for exploring North Iceland, and it works naturally as an aurora base because dark countryside begins close to town. You can spend the day exploring the region, return to Akureyri, and head back out after dark with a guide.

Dark countryside is close by. Akureyri itself has street lighting, so viewing from the centre isn't ideal for faint displays. Guided tours solve that problem by travelling outside the brighter built-up area and choosing a viewing location based on conditions that evening.

The setting is distinctly North Iceland. Eyjafjörður, mountains and snowy winter landscapes give aurora tours here a different atmosphere from excursions around Iceland's southwest. If Akureyri is already on your route, there's little reason to travel back to Reykjavik simply to book an aurora chase.

The tour choice is simple. Reykjavik wins on sheer variety, while Akureyri's smaller selection makes the decision fairly straightforward: photography-focused, second-chance flexibility, or a shorter excursion. You can compare the wider Iceland picture in our Northern Lights in Iceland guide.

Best Time to See the Northern Lights in Akureyri

The Northern Lights need darkness, which makes the darker months of the year the practical aurora season in Akureyri. Tours generally operate from late August or September into April, depending on the operator and available darkness.

November through February gives you very long nights and therefore plenty of potential viewing time. September, October and March can also be excellent months because the sky becomes properly dark while there are still more daylight hours available for sightseeing.

There isn't a month that guarantees better aurora on the night you happen to visit. Clear skies and sufficient auroral activity are the key ingredients, and Arctic weather can change quickly. If seeing the lights is a major reason for your trip, staying in Akureyri for several nights gives you more opportunities than relying on one fixed evening.

Where to Stay in Akureyri for a Northern Lights Trip

For most travellers taking a guided tour, staying in or close to central Akureyri is the easiest option. You'll have restaurants and other town amenities nearby, while many excursions offer pickup from selected accommodation or convenient points in town. If independent aurora viewing is more important to you, accommodation outside the brightest part of Akureyri can put darker skies closer to your door.

Frequently Asked Questions

📍 Is Akureyri a good place to see the Northern Lights?
Yes. Akureyri is a strong Northern Lights base in North Iceland because dark countryside is close to town and guided tours can travel away from local light pollution to search for clearer skies. Like anywhere else, sightings still depend on cloud cover and aurora activity.
🌌 Why choose Akureyri instead of Reykjavik for a Northern Lights tour?
Akureyri offers a quieter North Iceland setting and easy access to dark countryside, while Reykjavik has a much larger choice of tour styles and departures. If North Iceland is already part of your itinerary, Akureyri is a very convenient place to add an aurora tour without returning south.
🗓️ What is the best time to see the Northern Lights in Akureyri?
The practical season runs through the darker part of the year, generally from late August or September into April. November through February has especially long nights, while September, October and March can combine good darkness with more daylight for sightseeing. Clear skies and aurora activity ultimately matter more than choosing one specific month.
📸 Which Akureyri Northern Lights tour is best for photography?
Of the three tours compared here, the Northern Lights Photography Tour is the most photography-focused option. It's capped at 15 people and includes photographs of the Northern Lights if they're seen.
✅ What happens if I don't see the Northern Lights?
Policies vary by operator. The Northern Lights Tour from Akureyri featured above includes a second try, which is useful if conditions don't produce a sighting on your first outing. Always check the current terms on the booking page before reserving.
🌙 How many nights should I stay in Akureyri?
If the Northern Lights are an important part of your trip, more than one night is preferable. Multiple evenings give you another opportunity if cloud cover or weak aurora activity affects your first night.
