The 5 Best Northern Lights Tours in Kiruna

Compare our picks for Kiruna aurora tours — from photography-focused chases and Arctic dinners to an excursion towards Abisko National Park.

Kiruna makes a particularly practical base for a Northern Lights trip in Sweden. You get the convenience of the country's northernmost city, a wide choice of winter activities and accommodation, and quick access to the dark landscapes of Swedish Lapland once you leave the city lights behind.

The city sits around 200 kilometres north of the Arctic Circle and within the wider aurora-viewing region of northern Sweden. It is also the gateway to Jukkasjärvi and the famous ICEHOTEL, while Abisko lies roughly 100 kilometres to the west. That combination gives Kiruna a useful advantage: you can base yourself somewhere with restaurants, hotels and transport connections while still choosing tours that head deep into the surrounding countryside in search of clearer skies.

For this guide, we've organised five Kiruna Northern Lights experiences by the type of traveller they suit best. Prices, ratings and availability can change, so use the booking links to check the latest details for your dates.

Kiruna vs Abisko for the Northern Lights

Kiruna and Abisko are close enough to combine, but they make quite different bases. Kiruna is the better fit if you want a larger choice of accommodation, restaurants, transport connections and winter activities alongside your aurora hunting. It also puts Jukkasjärvi and the ICEHOTEL within easy reach.

Abisko, roughly 100 kilometres west of Kiruna, is smaller and more focused on the surrounding national park. Its location among the mountains often gives it clear skies when nearby areas are cloudy, which is why Abisko has such a strong reputation among aurora travellers.

You don't necessarily need to choose one and ignore the other. Several travellers use Kiruna as their main base and spend time in Abisko as part of the same trip. If Abisko itself is your priority, compare the dedicated experiences in our Abisko Northern Lights tours guide.

Why Choose Kiruna for a Northern Lights Trip?

It combines access with Arctic surroundings. Kiruna has an airport, rail connections, hotels and restaurants, yet tour operators can quickly take you away from artificial lighting and into open winter landscapes.

The tour choice is unusually varied. Aurora experiences here can include photography, snow activities, food, local storytelling and trips towards Abisko rather than only a standard coach chase.

Jukkasjärvi adds another reason to stay. The village is home to the world-famous ICEHOTEL and is also a hub for Sámi experiences. This makes it easy to build a broader Swedish Lapland itinerary around the Northern Lights rather than treating the aurora as the only activity.

Best Time to See the Northern Lights in Kiruna

The main Northern Lights season in Kiruna is September through March, when northern Sweden has the darkness required to see the aurora. Sightings can begin around late August and sometimes continue into April when conditions line up.

Kiruna's official destination information highlights September, October and March as months that can produce particularly strong and vivid displays. The deep-winter period brings much longer hours of darkness, including Kiruna's polar-night season, but darkness alone does not guarantee a sighting: you still need clear enough skies and aurora activity.

If seeing the lights is one of the main reasons for your trip, avoid building everything around a single night. Giving yourself several evenings creates more opportunities for the weather to cooperate. For the wider seasonal picture across Norway, Sweden, Finland and Iceland, see our best time to see the Northern Lights guide.

Where to Stay in Kiruna

Staying in Kiruna itself is convenient if you want easy access to restaurants, transport and tour pickups. For something more distinctive, nearby Jukkasjärvi is home to the ICEHOTEL and several Arctic experiences, while other properties around the area trade city convenience for darker surroundings.

Before booking accommodation, check where your chosen aurora tour picks up. A hotel included on the operator's pickup route can make a late winter evening considerably easier.

Frequently Asked Questions

✨ Can you see the Northern Lights in Kiruna?
Yes. Kiruna is in far northern Sweden, around 200 kilometres north of the Arctic Circle, and is a well-known base for aurora trips. For any particular night, you still need enough darkness, suitable aurora activity and sufficiently clear skies.
🗓️ What is the best time to see the Northern Lights in Kiruna?
The main season runs from September through March, although sightings can sometimes occur from late August into April. Kiruna's local destination information highlights September, October and March as months that can produce particularly strong displays.
🏔️ Is Kiruna or Abisko better for the Northern Lights?
They suit different trips. Kiruna gives you more city services, accommodation and a broad range of activities, while Abisko is especially known for its clear-sky microclimate and national-park setting. Because Abisko is roughly 100 kilometres west of Kiruna, many itineraries combine both.
🚐 Are Northern Lights tours in Kiruna worth it?
A guided tour is useful if you would rather not drive unfamiliar winter roads or interpret changing cloud and aurora forecasts yourself. Kiruna also has more specialised options, including photography tours, dinner experiences and excursions towards Abisko.
🌌 How many nights should I stay in Kiruna for the Northern Lights?
Several nights are preferable if the aurora is a priority because cloud cover can spoil any individual evening. No operator can guarantee a sighting, so giving yourself multiple opportunities is one of the simplest ways to reduce the effect of bad weather.
📍 How far is Abisko from Kiruna?
Abisko is roughly 100 kilometres west of Kiruna and can be reached by road or rail. Some Northern Lights tours departing Kiruna also travel towards the Abisko area.
