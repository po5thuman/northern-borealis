The Best Northern Lights Tours in Lapland

Compare aurora tours across Finnish Lapland, from Rovaniemi on the Arctic Circle to the snowy fells surrounding Levi.

Compare Lapland Tours

Finnish Lapland is one of Europe's great Northern Lights destinations. Long winter nights, snow-covered forests and easy access to dark countryside make northern Finland a natural base for an aurora trip — and travellers have an unusually wide choice of ways to experience it.

Two destinations stand out when planning Northern Lights tours in Lapland: Rovaniemi, the capital of Finnish Lapland and home of Santa Claus Village, and Levi, one of Finland's best-known ski resorts. Both offer guided aurora hunts, but the overall experience can be quite different.

This guide compares the two bases, highlights representative Lapland Northern Lights tours, and explains where to stay and when to visit. For a broader look at aurora travel across the country, see our guide to the Northern Lights in Finland.

Where Is Lapland in Finland?

Lapland is Finland's northernmost region, stretching from around the Arctic Circle all the way to the country's borders with Sweden, Norway and Russia. It covers a huge area of forests, fells, rivers and lakes, with settlements separated by vast stretches of sparsely populated wilderness.

For Northern Lights travellers, that geography is a major advantage. Once you move beyond the lights of the larger towns and resorts, there are extensive areas of dark countryside from which to watch the sky.

Most first-time visitors choose an established base rather than staying somewhere extremely remote. Rovaniemi and Levi are particularly useful because both combine accommodation, winter activities and organised Northern Lights excursions with easy access to darker surroundings.

Best Places for Northern Lights Tours in Lapland

There are many places to see the aurora across northern Finland, but Rovaniemi and Levi make particularly practical bases if you want organised tours, a good range of accommodation and other winter activities during the day.

Rovaniemi

Rovaniemi sits almost directly on the Arctic Circle and is the largest tourism hub in Finnish Lapland. It has a broad selection of organised aurora experiences, ranging from shorter evening excursions to longer-distance Northern Lights chases.

It is also home to Santa Claus Village, making Rovaniemi especially attractive for families and travellers who want to combine an aurora trip with the classic Lapland Christmas experience.

Levi

Levi lies farther north-west in the Kittilä area and offers a different type of Lapland trip. Best known as one of Finland's major ski resorts, Levi is surrounded by fells, forests and open countryside, giving visitors easy access to darker skies beyond the resort.

It is particularly attractive if you want to combine Northern Lights hunting with skiing, snowmobiling and other winter activities. Aurora experiences range from conventional minivan hunts to snowmobile excursions, photography trips and longer Northern Lights chases.

Where to Stay in Lapland for the Northern Lights

Where you stay can shape the entire trip. Rovaniemi provides the convenience of a small city and the widest range of visitor services, while Levi offers a compact resort environment centred around skiing and winter activities.

Where to Stay in Rovaniemi

Santa Claus Village & Lapland at Christmas

One of the biggest differences between Rovaniemi and Levi is Santa Claus Village. The attraction is located on the Arctic Circle just outside central Rovaniemi and is one of the reasons the city receives so many visitors during the winter.

Visitors can meet Santa Claus, cross the marked Arctic Circle and visit the Santa Claus Main Post Office. This makes Rovaniemi particularly convenient for families who want to combine the Northern Lights with the traditional Lapland Christmas experience.

Why See the Northern Lights in Lapland?

The biggest advantage of Finnish Lapland is not one particular town or attraction. It is the combination of northern latitude, long periods of winter darkness and relatively easy access to sparsely populated countryside.

There are many ways to experience the aurora. You can choose a dedicated minivan chase, photography tour, snowmobile trip, sledge ride or a private excursion depending on how much of the evening you want to devote to aurora hunting.

It works well as a complete winter holiday. Northern Lights viewing can be combined with skiing, husky experiences, reindeer activities, snowmobiling, sauna, winter hiking and other Arctic experiences.

Rovaniemi and Levi offer two different styles of trip. Rovaniemi combines city facilities and Santa Claus Village with access to the surrounding countryside, while Levi combines aurora tourism with one of Finland's major ski resorts.

Best Time to See the Northern Lights in Lapland

The Northern Lights require sufficiently dark skies, which means Lapland's practical aurora season falls during the darker part of the year. Viewing is generally possible from late August into early April, although conditions vary from night to night.

The deep winter months provide extremely long periods of darkness and the classic snow-covered Lapland scenery. September and March, meanwhile, still provide substantial darkness without being as deep into the Arctic winter.

No month guarantees a sighting. Aurora activity, cloud cover and local weather all matter, which is why staying for several nights gives you more opportunities than relying on a single evening.

Frequently Asked Questions

📍 Where is Lapland in Finland?
Lapland is Finland's northernmost region and extends across the Arctic Circle. Rovaniemi and Levi are two of its most popular bases for Northern Lights trips.
🌌 Can you see the Northern Lights in Lapland?
Yes. Finnish Lapland is one of Europe's major Northern Lights destinations. Its northern location, long winter nights and access to dark countryside make Rovaniemi, Levi and the surrounding region popular bases for aurora viewing.
⚖️ Is Rovaniemi or Levi better for Northern Lights tours?
Both are strong Northern Lights bases but suit different trips. Rovaniemi has a larger choice of tours and easy access to Santa Claus Village, while Levi combines aurora trips with skiing and easy access to the surrounding fell landscape.
🗓️ What is the best time to see the Northern Lights in Lapland?
Northern Lights viewing in Finnish Lapland is generally possible during the dark part of the year, from late August into early April. The core winter months provide very long hours of darkness, while autumn and spring can also offer good aurora viewing conditions.
🚐 Should I book a Northern Lights tour in Lapland?
A guided tour can be useful for travellers who do not have a car or local knowledge. Guides can travel away from town lights and adjust viewing locations according to weather and cloud conditions.
